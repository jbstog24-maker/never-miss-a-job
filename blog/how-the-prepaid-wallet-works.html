How the prepaid wallet works, and why there is no surprise bill

November 6, 2026 · Ringception AI

Your Ringception wallet is prepaid credit for calls and texts. You add money up front, see the balance in the portal, and pay only for the minutes and texts you actually use.

← Back to the blog

Nobody likes opening a bill that is bigger than they expected. For a small business, that kind of surprise messes with a tight budget.

That is why Ringception splits the cost into two clear parts. One part is your monthly plan. The other part is a prepaid wallet for calls and texts. This post explains how the wallet works, in plain words, so you always know where you stand.

Two parts, kept separate on purpose

Getting started is $44. That is $29 for setup plus $15 for your first month. After that, it is $15 a month per number, and the number is included. That covers your receptionist ready to answer, with your greeting, questions, and rules in place.

The wallet is different. It pays for the actual use, the calls your receptionist handles and the texts it sends for you. Calls are 17 cents a minute, billed per second. Texts are 5 cents each. The money comes from prepaid credit you add ahead of time, not from a bill that arrives later.

You add money before you spend it

Prepaid means exactly what it sounds like. You put money in the wallet first, and your receptionist draws from that balance as calls and texts happen. There is no tab running in the background and no invoice at the end of the month for usage you did not see coming.

You choose how much to add. If you want to start small while you get a feel for call volume, you can. If you know a busy season is coming, you can add more ahead of time. Either way, the decision is yours, made before the money is spent, not after.

Billed per second, so short calls stay cheap

Many phone services round every call up to a full minute. A quick call to confirm an address or move an appointment can end up costing the same as a long conversation. Ringception bills calls per second instead, so you pay for the time that was actually used.

That keeps quick check ins quick on the bill too. A longer, detailed call costs more than a short one, which is fair, because it did more work. You are paying for real conversations, measured honestly, down to the second.

You can always see the balance

A prepaid wallet only helps if you can see it. In your portal you can check your balance and see how it is being used, so a low balance is something you notice early, not something you discover after the fact.

When the balance gets low, you top it up in the portal. The control stays with you. Ringception does not quietly charge you for usage beyond the credit you added, and it does not turn a busy week into a surprise total. If you did not put it in the wallet, it cannot be spent from the wallet.

Busy weeks and quiet weeks even out honestly

Call volume is never perfectly steady. Some weeks the phone is quiet. Some weeks it rings all day while you are on a job, at a showing, or with a customer in front of you. With a prepaid wallet, a quiet week costs less and a busy week costs a little more, because the charge follows the work itself.

That also makes planning simpler. You can look at a normal week, get a feel for what your calls and texts tend to use, and keep a balance that fits your pace. No guessing about a fixed bundle you might not use, and no penalty for a week when business picks up.

Follow-Up Campaigns, if you add them on a business line, are separate again at $39 a month. They do not change how the wallet works. Calls and texts from that work still draw from the same prepaid credit, at the same plain rates, where you can see them.

What Ringception does about it

Ringception keeps the monthly plan and the prepaid wallet separate, shows your balance in the portal, bills calls per second at 17 cents a minute, and charges texts at 5 cents each from credit you added yourself. That is the whole design: clear rates, money added up front, and no surprise bill at the end of the month.
